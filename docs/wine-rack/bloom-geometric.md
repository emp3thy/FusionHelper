# Bloom - geometric front-plate pattern study

Second study, geometric only (the first, `bloom-patterns.md`, mixed families).
Page: `artifacts/wine-rack/bloom-geometric.html`: contact sheet of all twelve,
to-scale 3 x 3 renderings and one-tile close-ups, drawn from one geometry
routine (pitch 110.833, tile 110.53, seam 0.3, bore r 46, face r 50, collar
ring at r 52 unless noted).

## Ground rules used

Each pattern answers the seam in one of two ways: it **registers** to the
pitch (integer repeats per 110.83 mm in both axes, every tile carries the same
slice, lattice continues through the gap) or it **centres on the node** (one
quarter of a figure per tile, mirror-matched across the seam). Each resolves at
the bore with a 1-2 mm plain band and a ring in the second colour, so no motif
is cut mid-shape by the flare. Two usable registrations: sevenths (15.83 mm)
and eighths (13.85 mm) of the pitch; 7 x 8 approximates a triangular / hex
lattice to one percent.

Geometric facts per pattern:

- Girih: two squares of circumradius 24 on each node (diagonal point 2.2 mm
  clear of the collar); 1.5 mm rails 1.2 mm inside every edge make each seam a
  3.3 mm double strap. Collar r 51.5.
- Seigaiha: circles r 13.85 at 27.7 centres, rows 6.93 apart, half offset;
  translates not mirrors, so arcs cross the seams at the same angle both sides.
- Chevron: columns P/4 wide, stripes 2.6 mm at 45 deg on a P/16 pitch; folds
  coincide with tile edges. True 2:1 herringbone has no square period.
- Fret: band 8.75-17.5 mm off the node, unit 1.75, one hook per half-side,
  corner dot; rail continuous across the seam, hooks mirror. A key border round
  the bore does not fit the 5.3 mm mid-side annulus.
- Blocks: cube width P/7, row P/8 (edge 9.24, 1 percent off regular); H2D
  third tone from a 0.6 mm relief on the right face.
- Tessera: side P/7, row P/8; down-triangles marble, up-triangles walnut.
- Truchet: cell P/8 = 13.85, arcs r 6.93, one seed, four rotations; arcs always
  end at cell-edge midpoints so every seam is crossed cleanly. Converts to a
  through-cut lattice for the lit variant.
- Quatrefoil: four circles r 11 at 11 from the node, 1.5 outline, 3 mm boss;
  lobes lie on the seams (visible axis line through eight lobes per node).
- Sashiko: 45 deg lines with intercepts every P/6 = 18.47, stitch period 4.354
  (three per lattice span), dash offset puts every crossing in a gap; ~600
  first-layer islands of 2.9 x 1.2 per tile.
- Bauhaus: disc r 5.5, rings 11.5-15.5 and 19.5-23.5 on every node; one
  terracotta quarter disc r 24 per tile, tiles rotated in turn.
- Stepped fan: arcs r 6/11/16/21, 3 mm, spanning 84/72/60/48 deg; seams fall in
  the notches of the four-pointed stepped star; stepped collar 2.0 + 1.0 mm.
- Moire: 1 mm rings on a 3 mm pitch about each node (r 3-48) and each bore
  (r 55-80); node rings shared by four tiles, bore rings mirror at mid-side
  seams (a visible crease). Inlay only; never relief.

## Top three

1. **Girih** (13). The geometry of the grid itself: stars on the nodes, bottles
   in the crosses, seams turned into straps. Two colours, 1.5 mm lines, zero
   extra material.
2. **Truchet** (13). Best seam-hiding of the geometric set, two colours, nine
   identical tiles reading as nine different ones through rotation; the only
   one that converts directly to a through-cut lit lattice.
3. **Blocks** (13). The showpiece on an H2C (three tones, strongest optical
   depth, bores read as drilled holes). On the H2D the third tone is relief and
   flattens in low light; print one tile both ways first.

Close behind: Stepped fan (dark deco room), Fret (classical two-colour),
Chevron (quiet, single-colour relief option).

All twelve are flat-inlay candidates, so one face-down test tile in the chosen
filament pair settles the surface question for the whole page.
