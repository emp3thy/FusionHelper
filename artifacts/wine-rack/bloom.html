Wine rack for a 335 mm cube · pretty direction · slug bloom

Bloomnine bottles, one flower at every crossing

A plain PETG-CF skeleton of flat rings on square rods carries the bottles. Walnut-wood tiles with marble leaves pin to its face and carry nothing but the look. Two colours, one nozzle each, no supports, no glue.

Rev 2

What changed. Each cell now has three rings on four pass-through 10 mm rods instead of two rings on integral posts. In rev 1 the front ring sat 256 mm from the bottle base, past the shoulder of a 320 mm Bordeaux, so only the rear ring touched the body and the bottle rested on its shoulder taper. The new middle ring (contact 156-172 from the base) and the rear ring (0-16) both bear on the body either side of the centre of gravity, so bottles lie level. Rods print lying flat; the 240 mm towers are gone. Cost: one ring and four rods more per cell, about 0.5 kg and 6 h over the whole rack.

1 · Concept

The decoration is a separate part

Every reference in the pretty direction (GustaVino's Gaudi cells, the Alessi Barkcellar, the Voronoi hex modules, edge-lit acrylic racks) puts the ornament on the load path and pays for it in material, supports or sag. The research's strongest advice is to split the two: a plain creep-resistant skeleton, and a decorative skin in a filament chosen for its surface. Bloom does exactly that.

  • Skeleton. Each cell is three flat square rings 16 mm thick (hoop in XY, bottle axis vertical on the bed) on four 10 mm square rods. The end rings carry 12 mm blind pockets, the middle ring square through holes; every rod has a tick groove where the middle ring sits. Rings print flat, rods print flat, nothing overhangs; the one bridge is the 10.2 mm pocket roof inside the front ring.
  • Skin. A 110.5 mm bezel tile, 8 mm thick, pins onto the front ring. It carries the 33.7 degree entry flare, eight half-leaves and a collar in a second filament (1.2 mm raised, vertical flanks), and an 11 x 3.2 mm rebate on its back for a 10 mm COB strip. It can be reprinted in any colour without touching the structure.
  • Why leaves at the nodes. Between a 92 mm bore and a 110.8 mm pitch the annulus is only 5 mm wide at mid-side; there is no field for a motif around the bottle. The field is at the grid crossings, so each tile corner carries two quarter-leaves and four tiles complete one flower. The 0.3 mm seam reads as the midrib.
  • Ties. Double-dovetail keys slide front-to-back into a groove on every ring face, at all three planes. 36 keys turn nine cells into one block and hold every middle ring to its neighbours; the tiles then cover the front groove mouths.

Print the set, push the stub into the pocket and through the hole (thumb pressure, no mallet), pick the key that slides and holds against a shake, then set rod_gap and dt_clr. If a pin rattles, lower hole_comp by 0.1. Nothing on the full plates is printed until the coupon says so.

Elephant-foot compensation 0.15 mm in the slicer handles every bed-side edge; the model carries no bed chamfers so the rings keep their full bearing face. Only pocket mouths and rod ends are chamfered in the model.

6 · Filament and settings

Walnut and marble on a carbon frame

Skeleton: Bambu PETG-CF (primary)

  • Colour irrelevant; black hides best through the 0.3 mm seams.
  • 0.6 mm hardened HF nozzle, 0.3 mm layers, 250-260 C, bed 70 C.
  • Textured PEI with a thin glue-stick release layer; chamber lid closed, heater off.
  • 4 walls, 15 percent gyroid, 5 top/bottom layers, Arachne on.
  • 5 mm brim on the ring plates; rods with the seam aligned to one edge, no brim needed.
  • Dry 65 C / 8 h (AMS 2 Pro does this). Alternative: PETG HF at 5 walls, about 40 percent less stiff, GBP 18/kg.

Tiles: Bambu PLA Wood Black Walnut + PLA Marble (primary)

  • Nozzle 1 walnut for the tile body, nozzle 2 marble for leaves and collar. Two colours, one per nozzle: near zero purge, tiles inside the 300 x 320 zone.
  • 0.4 mm hardened nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 210-220 C, bed 55 C, chamber vented.
  • 4 walls, 20 percent gyroid, ironing on the relief tops, seam aligned to a tile corner.
  • Dry 55 C / 8 h. Wood PLA is mildly abrasive; the stock hardened nozzle is fine.
  • Alternatives: Fiberlogy FiberWood (sands and stains like timber) with Panchroma Marble; or the lit version below.

Lit variant (optional electronics)

Print the tiles in Bambu PETG Translucent with the relief in PETG HF white (265 C, 0.1 mm layers for clarity). Lay a 10 mm wide, 24 V warm-white COB strip (about 1.5 mm thick) in the 11 x 3.2 mm rebates that run along the top and bottom of every tile; across a row the rebates line up into one channel behind the seams, so three strips of 330 mm light the whole face from behind the midribs. Feed wires exit at the sides into the 1.25 mm fit gap. A 12-24 V 2 A supply at the back of the cube is plenty. Without the strip the rebates are invisible and cost 7 g per tile.

7 · Assembly and retention

Half an hour, no tools

  1. Print and test the coupon set; adjust rod_gap, dt_clr, fit_push, hole_comp if needed, re-bundle, re-gate.
  2. Print plates R1 to R7 and F to H.
  3. For each cell: push four rods into the rear ring's pockets, tick end up (the tick is 152 mm from the rod's rear end, 112 from its front end). Slide the middle ring down the rods until its rear face sits on the ticks. Press the front ring onto the rod ends, pockets first, pins facing out. A drop of CA in each middle-ring hole is optional.
  4. Lay the nine cells on a table front-face up in a 3 x 3. Slide a key into every pair of facing grooves at the front plane (12), stand the block up and do the middle plane (12), then the rear plane (12). Keys go in from the face, flush.
  5. Press a tile onto each front ring: four pins, one way. If lighting, lay the strips in the rebates first and run the leads out at the sides.
  6. Stick four 20 mm rubber feet under the bottom rear and front rings; slide the rack into the cube. The 332.5 envelope leaves 1.25 mm per side; at 20 kg loaded it goes nowhere. If the cube is deeper than 280, push the rack to the front edge so the bottles stay reachable.

Tiles cover the front groove mouths, the cube back blocks the rear ones; no key can walk out. To change the look, pull the tiles and print another set: 340 g, three plates.

9 · Risks and open questions

What could still go wrong

  1. Cube depth is unknown. At rack_d 280 a 320 bottle protrudes 40 mm. If the cube is under 300 deep or the look must be flush, set rack_d to 250: rods shorten to 234 and the middle ring stays where it is.
  2. Three fits decide everything. The 0.1 mm rod, 0.25 mm key and 0.1 mm pin clearances are printed-tolerance guesses within the brief's bands. The coupon set exists so the first full plate is never the test.
  3. Middle ring held by friction. It is located by the rod ticks and tied to its neighbours by 12 keys, but nothing clamps it to the rods. If a ring walks under a knock, a drop of CA in each through hole fixes it; the outer cells' free faces have no neighbour key, so check those first.
  4. Pocket roofs in the front ring. Each is a 10.2 mm bridge printed at 12 mm height; PETG-CF bridges this cleanly but inspect the underside of the first ring_f plate.
  5. Burgundy and Champagne shoulders. The middle ring at 156-172 meets the start of their shoulder slope (about 1 degree nose-down). Set mid_z 125 for a Burgundy-heavy cellar.
  6. PLA tiles near heat. Walnut PLA softens at 57 C. Beside an oven or in a sunny window, print the tiles in ASA (matte, lower surface detail) or the translucent PETG variant; the skeleton does not care.
  7. Creep. Skeleton stress is around 0.02 MPa in bearing and the hoops are loaded in XY. No PLA carries load anywhere. Expected sag over years: none measurable.
  8. Prestige Champagne and magnums do not fit. That is the 3 x 3 trade; a 96 mm bore is one parameter away (with dt_depth 2.5).
  9. Unverified model. The rev 2 script passed the offline gate but has not run in Fusion; its runtime checks (profile counts, volume deltas, 16-edge chamfer picks, bed fit) stop it loudly if a feature misbehaves.
