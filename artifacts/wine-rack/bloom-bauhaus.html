Bloom wine rack · companion note 3 · Bauhaus front-plate variants

Nine squares, nine circlesten Bauhaus fronts for the same tiles

The Bauhaus direction, worked through. The bottle is the blue circle of Kandinsky's trio; the tile is the square; the lobes carry the triangle, the quarter-disc and the bar. Because each of the nine tiles is its own print, the rack can be nine different two-colour tiles from a five-colour palette on an H2D with no purge at all, and that is used here as a compositional tool.

0 · Vocabulary, palette, rules

What is being drawn

Tile 110.53 mm square, pitch 110.83, seam 0.3, face opening 100 mm. The decorative field is four corner lobes (28 mm on the diagonal, 5.3 mm at mid-side) and a collar. All ten variants are flat two-colour-per-layer inlays printed face-down: the face is the first layer on textured PEI, every colour edge is crisp to 0.8 mm, and no material is added. Shapes stay outside r 53.5 from the bore centre, so nothing is cut mid-form by the flare.

Per-tile colour. On the H2D each tile is body + one accent (two nozzles, zero purge), and different tiles can use different pairs. The whole rack can therefore show four or five colours while every plate stays two-colour. Three or more colours on one tile need the H2C (8 s hotend swaps on the first two layers only). Each variant states which.

Seams and nodes. Figures either sit on the nodes (one quarter per tile, so adjacent tiles' colours meet and make the composition), run along the seams as bars that swallow them, or frame each tile deliberately so the seam reads as a drawn gap.

Palette

redyellowblueblackoff-white

Primaries: Bambu PLA Matte (tiles carry no load, so the weak layer bonding does not matter) in Scarlet Red, Lemon Yellow, Marine Blue, Charcoal and Ivory White; PETG HF white or black if a satin rather than matte face is wanted. Muted: Polymaker Panchroma Matte (ex-PolyTerra) in the nearest brick, ochre / mustard, slate grey and cream; walnut and marble stand in for black and white on the frame-based variants (Breuer, Seal, Stijl).

Contact sheet

01 · kandinsky, point and line to plane (1926)

TrioBlue circle, red square, yellow triangle; the bottle is the circle

Source
Kandinsky's colour-form correspondence (Bauhaus questionnaire 1923): yellow triangle, red square, blue circle.
Made
Flat inlay, face-down.
Colours
4 per tile (H2C). Rack: 4. For the H2D see Rotation, which is this idea split one accent per tile.
Filament
PLA Matte Ivory White body, Marine Blue ring, Scarlet Red square, Lemon Yellow triangle.
Bore
Blue ring 3.5 mm at r 50.75 to 54.25: the ring is the circle, the bottle fills it.
Seams
Square quarters (14 mm) are node-centred; with rotation the node shows one to four red quarters. Triangles stop 1.5 mm from the edges.
Min feature
3.5 mm ring; triangle tip 1.5 mm from edge, 90 degree corner.
Extra per tile
0 g, +6 min (four colours on the first two layers).

The literal reading of the brief, and the one most people will recognise as Bauhaus from across the room. Rotating identical tiles turns one composition into nine: at some nodes four red quarters close into a full 28 mm square, at others a single quarter faces a triangle. The seams run through the squares, which Kandinsky's own grids also do, but they are visible.

02 · layout: 3 x 3 colour rotation (H2D)

RotationEach tile one accent; red, yellow and blue cycle along the diagonals

Source
Kandinsky's trio distributed by Itten's colour-contrast exercises: one hue per tile, diagonal repetition.
Made
Flat inlay, face-down; three tile files (red, yellow, blue), three plates of three.
Colours
2 per tile. Rack: 4. H2D native, zero purge.
Filament
Ivory White body on all nine; one accent spool per plate.
Bore
Blue tiles: ring 3.5 mm. Red and yellow tiles: no ring, the bore is a plain hole with a 1 mm clearance band.
Seams
Red quarter squares and yellow triangles sit in opposite corners of their tiles so that, along each diagonal, like meets like at the node and the figures complete.
Min feature
14 mm squares, 24 mm triangles, 3.5 mm ring.
Extra per tile
0 g, +3 min.

Printability●●●●●
Hides seams●●●○○

This is the H2D's answer to Trio. The rack reads as a four-colour composition, every print is a two-colour print with one nozzle per colour, and three plates of three identical tiles is the simplest possible production. The only compromise is that six of nine bottles get no ring.

03 · albers, homage to the square (1950 onward)

HomageNested squares on every node, offset downward as Albers set them

Source
Josef Albers, Homage to the Square: three or four nested squares, bottom-weighted, tonal families.
Made
Flat inlay, face-down.
Colours
4 per tile (H2C). H2D fallback: outer and inner square in the accent, middle square in the body colour (a square ring and a dot), 2 per tile.
Filament
Ivory White body; yellow, red, black squares. In the muted set: cream, ochre, brick, slate, which is closer to Albers' own tonal sets.
Bore
Black ring 2.5 mm at r 52.5; the outer square's corner is 3.4 mm clear of it.
Seams
Both seams cross every Homage through its centre; the downward offset makes the lower tiles carry more of each figure.
Min feature
Inner square 12 mm; 1.5 mm steps between squares.
Extra per tile
0 g, +6 min (H2C) / +3 min (H2D fallback).

Printability●●●●○
Hides seams●●●○○

Albers is the quietest Bauhaus voice and the one that works best in the muted palette: the three tones only need to be close, and matte filaments in cream, ochre and brick give exactly the dry surface his paintings have. The seams through the centre of every square are the cost; Albers' squares are painted edge to edge and a cross through them is not in the source.

04 · bayer, quarter-circles and discs; layout: single diagonal accent

BayerA large and a small quarter-disc per tile, red on the diagonal, black elsewhere

Source
Herbert Bayer's 1920s poster and cover geometry (Bauhaus Dessau, Kandinsky exhibition poster 1926): quarter-circles, half-discs and a ring, asymmetrically placed.

Seams
Quarter-discs are node-centred; rotation makes each node a different Bayer corner (two discs of different radii, a disc and a gap, and so on).
Min feature
13 mm small disc, 2.5 mm ring.
Extra per tile
0 g, +3 min.

Bayer's compositions are built from exactly the pieces this tile has room for, and the single diagonal of red is the most economical colour statement on the page: three red tiles, six black, one body spool. Where quarter-discs of different sizes meet at a node the result looks designed, not tiled, and the bores read as the largest discs in the set.

05 · moholy-nagy, diagonals and overlapping discs

MoholyDiagonal bars that close into diamonds around every node; two discs overlapping in a third tone

Source
Moholy-Nagy's paintings and photograms of the 1920s (A XX, Composition A II): diagonals, transparent overlapping discs, black bars.
Made
Flat inlay, face-down. The overlap is a fourth colour (orange) printed as its own region. On the H2D: bar and disc in one accent, the small disc as a body-coloured hole in the large one.
Colours
5 per tile (H2C); 2 per tile on the H2D (loses the overlap).
Filament
Ivory White, Charcoal bars, Scarlet Red and Lemon Yellow discs, an orange matte for the lens. Translucent PETG discs are the literal option on a lit rack.
Bore
Black ring 2.5 mm at r 52.5; the bars pass 1.9 mm outside the clearance band.
Seams
Bars leave each tile at 30 mm from the corner on both edges and continue on the neighbour, so the seams are crossed by a bar at every node; the diamonds hide them.
Min feature
3 mm bar; the orange lens is 4.6 mm wide at its widest.
Extra per tile
0 g, +7 min (H2C) / +3 min (H2D).
Looks●●●●○
Printability●●●○○
Hides seams●●●●○

The diagonal is the one Bauhaus move that the square grid of tiles resists, so it is used where the grid allows it: across the lobes, where four tiles' bars close into a diamond that frames each node. The overlapping discs are the Moholy signature; printing the overlap as a mixed colour is honest about the medium (filament is not translucent) and reads correctly from a metre away.

06 · stolzl, woven stripe blocks

StolzlHorizontal stripe sets with a shifted block, the way a wall hanging steps

Source
Gunta Stolzl's weaving workshop pieces (Schlitzgobelin 1927-28, the 5 Choere hangings): stripe bands of unequal width, colour-swapped blocks, slit boundaries.
Made
Flat inlay, face-down.
Colours
3 per tile (H2C); 2 on the H2D (the second stripe colour becomes the body, so the block reads as a slit).
Filament
Ivory White body, Scarlet Red and Marine Blue stripes. Muted: cream with brick and slate, or walnut with marble and ochre, which is closest to the original wool.
Bore
Clearance disc r 54 in the body colour, black ring 2.5 mm at r 52.5: the bottle interrupts the weave like a hole cut in cloth.
Seams
Stripes run through the column seams; the stripe set has period P/2, so the row seams fall on a stripe boundary.
Min feature
2 mm stripes; 10 mm block shift.
Extra per tile
0 g, +5 min.
Looks●●●●○
Printability●●●●○
Hides seams●●●●○

The weaving workshop was the Bauhaus's most commercially successful, and its stripe language sits better in a kitchen than the painters' primaries do. The stripes carry the eye across the column seams and the block boundaries give the front a rhythm the bores do not break. In walnut and marble with an ochre third stripe it is the warmest front on the page.

08 · breuer, tubular steel; layout: one odd tile

BreuerA bent-tube frame around each tile, a cantilever arc in one corner, the centre tile inverted

Source
Marcel Breuer's B3 (Wassily) and B33 cantilever: a continuous bent tube drawn as a rounded rectangle, with a circle (the seat) inside it.
Made
Flat inlay, face-down; or the tube as a 1 mm half-round relief, which is closest to the source.
Colours
2 per tile (frame only) on the H2D; 3 with the red arc (H2C). Rack: 3 with the inverted centre tile.
Filament
Ivory White body with Charcoal tube (centre tile reversed); PETG HF black has the satin of chromed steel. Muted: walnut body, marble tube.
Bore
No separate ring: the tube passes 2 mm outside the face opening at mid-side, so the bore is the circle inside the frame, as the seat is inside the B3.
Seams
Framed deliberately: two tubes and the gap make a 4.3 mm double line along every seam. The seam is part of the drawing.
Min feature
2.6 mm tube; 2 mm body margin to the flare at mid-side.
Extra per tile
0 g, +3 min (inlay); +1.4 g, +5 min (relief tube).

The furniture answer. A tube frame per tile turns the nine-piece front into nine framed objects, which is how Breuer's chairs sit in a room anyway, and the one black tile in the middle is the simplest odd-tile layout: it costs nothing and gives the rack a centre. If the relief option is chosen, a half-round tube catches light along its length and looks like steel from any angle.

10 · de stijl contrast; layout: colour fields

Source
Mondrian and van Doesburg: black bars on a white ground with primary fields. Included as the strict grid against which the Bauhaus variants read as composed.

Every seam sits inside a 3 mm bar (the bar is on one side only, the gap on the bar's edge). The outer right and bottom edges of the rack get bars too, from the last column and row.

3 mm bars; 2.3 mm body between bar and flare at mid-side.
Extra per tile
0 g, +3 min.

Printability●●●●●

The one variant where the seams disappear completely: each sits under a black bar, and the bars are the design. Using the tile body as the colour field is the cleanest use of nine separate prints, because a red tile is just the same file with a different spool in nozzle one. It is De Stijl rather than Bauhaus strictly, which is why it is last; it is also the highest-scoring front on the page.

All variants are flat inlays: 0 g extra per tile; minutes are colour-change overhead on the first two layers against the 38 g, 48 min base tile (H2C swaps 8 s each; H2D is two nozzles, so two colours cost nothing beyond the prime line). Bracketed values are the H2D version. Col / rack counts the body colours too. Nothing has been printed; ratings are a designer's estimate.

Which layouts need an H2C

  • H2D, zero purge: Rotation, Bayer, Stijl, Breuer without the red arc. Any layout where each tile is body + one accent, including nine tiles in nine different pairs.
  • H2C only: Trio (four colours on one tile), Moholy's orange overlap, Stolzl's two stripe colours, Schlemmer's two-colour figure, Seal's red disc, Homage in three tones. Each has a stated two-colour fallback.
  • H2D with AMS purge (not recommended): any of the H2C variants at three colours costs roughly 5 g and 4 min per colour change per tile; on nine tiles that is an extra spool's worth of waste for a first-layer effect.
12 · Recommendation

Top three

1. Stijl

Seams gone under the bars, five colours on the rack from two-colour prints, four files that differ only by spool. The strictest grid on the page and the one that uses the nine separate tiles most honestly. Switch to the muted set and walnut bars if primaries are too loud.

2. Bayer

The most Bauhaus-looking front that the H2D prints natively: quarter-discs that compose differently at every node, a ring on every bottle, and one red diagonal as the whole colour budget. Three red tiles, six black, done.

3. Breuer

The furniture reading. A tube frame per tile makes the seams a drawn double line, the inverted centre tile gives the rack a focus for free, and as a half-round relief it is the only variant that looks like a material rather than a print.

If the H2C is available, Trio is the literal brief and Stolzl the warmest; Seal is the quiet one for a room that must stay calm. Rotation is Trio for the H2D and the easiest production run on the page.

Before modelling: print one Ivory White tile with Charcoal bars (the Stijl file) and one with a Scarlet Red quarter-disc (the Bayer file), face-down, and look at the textured-PEI finish on the matte filaments in the room. Every variant on this page lives or dies on that one surface.
