Quiet Nine

A nine-bottle insert that sits at the back of the cube and lets the bottles do the showing. Matte grey, 164 mm deep, no frame, no fasteners, nothing to look at.

Concept

An unobtrusive rack has one job: hold the bottles level and get out of the way. The KALLAX-class cube is about 390 mm deep and a bottle is 300 to 330 mm long, so a rack does not need to reach the front of the cube at all. Quiet Nine is a short cradle, 164 mm deep, pushed to the back. From the front you see nine shoulders and necks; the lattice sits 150 mm behind them, in shadow, matte grey.

The lattice is nine identical cells. Each cell is two thin rings, one at the back with a stop plate so a bottle cannot be pushed out of an open-backed cube, one near the front with a 45° flared entry, joined by four slim corner ribs. Every ring prints flat, hoop in the XY plane, so the bottle load is carried by continuous perimeters and the rows stack ring-on-ring straight down to the cube floor. There is no outer frame: the nine cells key into one slab, and the cube walls box it in.

The front face is the thinnest wall the pitch allows. At 110.83 mm pitch and 94 mm bore the wall between bottles is 8.4 mm, and the 5 mm flare takes it down to a 3.4 mm land at the face, so the lattice reads as a line drawing rather than a grid of blocks.

Modules and plate plan

Everything prints flat, ring face on the bed, no supports. Two halves per plate axis (221.7 mm plus brim) is the limit, so four halves per plate. Rates follow the brief: 0.4 nozzle at 0.2 mm, about 60 g/h real; a 0.6 nozzle at 0.3 mm takes the rack to about 14 h.

The coupon carries three key sockets at dt_gap −0.1 / 0 / +0.1 and three peg bores at peg_gap −0.1 / 0 / +0.1, with one key and one loose peg. Choose the pair that pushes home by thumb and holds its own weight upside down, then set the two parameters and print the halves.

Filament and settings

Primary: Bambu ASA in grey (or white to match a white cube, black for a dark one). Naturally matte, will not yellow by a window, HDT 100 °C, and the parts are small enough that chamber warping is a non-issue. Alternative with less fuss: Prusament PETG Matte or Bambu PETG HF in a dark colour, same settings minus the chamber.

Assembly and retention

  1. Print plate 0. Pick the key socket and peg bore that push home by thumb; set dt_gap and peg_gap, re-run the script, slice the halves.
  2. Lay the nine rear halves stop-plate-down in a 3 × 3, faces touching. Drop a key into each facing socket pair from above (twelve keys). Press flush.
  3. Lay the nine front halves flare-down in a 3 × 3 the same way. Twelve more keys.
  4. Lift the front slab, turn it pegs-down, lower it onto the rear slab. Thirty-six pegs find their bores; press down evenly with a flat board. No glue needed; a drop of CA on each peg makes it permanent.
  5. Stick the felt pads. Slide the slab into the cube flare forward and push it to the back. The cube walls hold it; the felt holds its position along the depth.
  6. Load bottles base-first until they meet the stop plate. From the front, the rack is gone.

Risks and open questions

  • Cube depth unknown. The design assumes a 390 mm cube and a rack at the back. In a 335 mm niche it still works (bottles end flush, flutes protrude 25), but the lattice is then only 170 mm from the face and more visible. half_d is the lever; keep the front ring under the body.
  • Front support on a sloping shoulder. Short-bodied Burgundy and Champagne shapes may have started tapering by 155 mm from the base. The 4 mm rear drop covers up to 4 mm of radius loss; a bottle with more taper there sits level rather than neck-up. Verify with the fattest short bottle in the cellar before printing all nine.
  • Fit coupon first. 36 pegs and 24 keys share one clearance each; a 0.1 mm error repeats 60 times. The coupon exists for this.
  • Slab shrink across 332.5 mm. 0.3 % is 1 mm; the 1.25 mm gap and the loose peg fit absorb it, but print all halves from the same spool and plate temperature.
  • ASA chamber. The parts are small, but ASA still wants a 55–60 °C preheat and glue. If that is a nuisance, PETG matte prints the same geometry with no drama.
  • Creep. Not governing at these stresses, but the rings rest on their 8.4 mm walls for years; the 12 mm ring depth is deliberate headroom.
  • Stop plate mass. 25 g per cell is the single largest discretionary item. Four lugs instead of a full annulus would save ~150 g; left solid for bed adhesion and a clean rear face.
