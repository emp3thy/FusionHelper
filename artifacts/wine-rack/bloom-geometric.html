Bloom wine rack · companion note 2 · geometric front-plate patterns

Twelve geometriesfor the same nine tiles

A second pass on the Bloom front, geometric only. Every pattern is either registered to the 110.83 mm pitch so it runs through the seams unbroken, or built on the grid nodes so four tiles complete one figure. All drawings are to scale from one geometry routine; the collar and the 100 mm face opening are drawn so you can see exactly where each pattern meets the bottle.

0 · Ground rules

How a geometry survives nine tiles and nine holes

The tile is 110.53 mm square, the pitch 110.83 (0.3 mm seam), the face opening 100 mm. Two things break a geometric pattern here: the seam, which cuts straight through every grid node, and the bore, which removes a 100 mm disc from the middle of every 110 mm tile, leaving 5.3 mm at mid-side and 28 mm on the diagonal.

Each pattern below answers both. For the seam it does one of two things: registers to the pitch (an integer number of repeats per 110.83 mm in both axes, so every tile carries the same slice and the lattice runs on across the gap), or centres on the node (one quarter of a figure per tile, mirror-matched across the seam). For the bore it resolves at the collar: a plain clearance band 1 to 2 mm wide, then a ring in the second colour, so no motif is cut off mid-shape by the flare.

Making: relief up (face-up print, 0.6 to 2 mm, ironed tops) or flat inlay (face-down, first layer on textured PEI, two colours side by side on the H2D, more on the H2C, zero extra material, no layer lines on the face). Minimum feature 0.8 mm; most patterns here use 1.0 to 1.75 mm lines. Through-cut is noted where a pattern suits the lit PETG variant.

01 · islamic star / girih

GirihEight-point strapwork stars on the nodes, rails along every seam

Family
Islamic geometric, khatem sulemani drawn as strapwork (lines, not filled pieces).
Made
Flat inlay, face-down; also good at 0.8 mm relief.
Registration
Node-centred: two squares of circumradius 24 mm (one axis-aligned, one at 45 degrees) centred on each node; the diagonal point stops 2.2 mm short of the collar. Rails 1.5 mm wide, 1.2 mm inside each edge. Collar ring 2 mm at r 51.5.
Colours
2 (H2C: rails in a third colour).
Filament
Walnut with marble lines; PETG-CF black with PETG HF white for a sharper reading.
Min feature
1.5 mm lines; smallest enclosed field 3 mm (the star-point triangles).
Extra per tile
0 g, +4 min.
Looks●●●●●
Printability●●●●○
Hides seams●●●●○

Star-and-cross is the one classical geometry that already contains this rack: stars at the lattice points, a cross-shaped field between them, and our bottles sit in the crosses. Drawn as strapwork rather than filled tiles it stays two-colour, and the rails give the seams a reason to exist: each 0.3 mm gap runs down the middle of a 3.3 mm double strap. The star is cut by nothing; the straps are cut only by the collar, at right angles.

Min feature
1.2 mm arcs, 2.2 mm between arcs; the visible crescent of each scale is 6.9 mm tall.
Extra per tile
0 g, +6 min (long first-layer path).
Looks●●●●○

Hides seams●●●●○

Seigaiha only works if it never stops, so it is drawn as translates (every tile carries the same slice) rather than mirrored at the seams; the arcs cross the gaps at the same angle on both sides. Four scales per pitch is the coarsest grain that still reads as waves from across a room, and it gives the column seams a scale boundary to hide in.

03 · herringbone / chevron

ChevronFour zigzag columns per pitch, stripes every 6.93 mm

Family
Parquet chevron (point-matched herringbone).
Made
Flat inlay, face-down; or single-colour relief (stripes raised 0.8) for a carved-panel look.
Registration
Pitch-registered: columns 27.7 mm wide, stripes 2.6 mm wide at 45 degrees on a 6.93 mm vertical pitch; fold lines coincide with the tile edges. Plain 2 mm band then a 2 mm collar at r 52.
Colours
2 (or 1 as relief).
Filament
Walnut with marble (two-tone parquet); walnut alone as relief.
Min feature
2.6 mm stripes, 4.3 mm gaps; stripe ends at the folds are 90 degree points, fine at 0.4.
Extra per tile
0 g, +4 min (relief: +3.1 g, +7 min).
Looks●●●●○
Printability●●●●●
Hides seams●●●●○

A true 2:1 block herringbone has no square period, so this is the chevron variant: it folds exactly on the tile edges, which makes the column seams read as the folds a parquet floor has anyway. The stripes cross the row seams without a kink. The collar cuts the stripes at every angle, which is why a plain band sits between them and the ring.

04 · greek key

FretA square Greek-key frame on every node, 35 mm across

Family
Greek key / meander, as a corner frame.
Made
Flat inlay, face-down; equally good at 1 mm relief.
Registration
Node-centred: band from 8.75 to 17.5 mm off the node, unit 1.75 mm (lines and gaps), one hook per half-side, a square dot in the corner cell. The frame's outer corner is 1.5 mm clear of the collar on the diagonal. Collar ring 2 mm at r 52.
Colours
2.
Filament
PETG-CF black with PETG HF white (the classic); walnut with marble.
Min feature
1.75 mm lines and 1.75 mm gaps; nothing finer.
Extra per tile
0 g, +3 min.
Looks●●●●○
Printability●●●●●
Hides seams●●●●○

A key border around each bore does not fit: the band at mid-side is 5.3 mm and a key needs five line widths. So the key frames the node instead, where there is room for a 10 mm band, and the bore gets a plain ring. Each tile holds an L of the frame; the rail runs straight across the seam and the hooks reverse direction there, as the hooks on a real fret do at every corner.

05 · tumbling blocks

BlocksIsometric cubes, seven across and eight rows per pitch

Family
Rhombille / tumbling blocks (quilt and marble-floor classic).
Made
H2C: flat inlay in three colours. H2D: two colours plus a 0.6 mm relief on the third face (the right rhombus is raised walnut and reads darker or lighter with the room light).
Registration
Pitch-registered: cube width P/7 = 15.83, row pitch P/8 = 13.85 (edge 9.24, one percent off a true regular cube). Plain 2 mm band then a 2 mm collar at r 52.
Colours
3 on H2C; 2 + relief on H2D.
Filament
Walnut, marble and (H2C) a matte mid-tone such as terracotta or cream; or PETG-CF black, white and grey.
Min feature
Rhombus short diagonal 9.2 mm; acute corners 60 degrees, fine. Relief faces 0.6 mm high need 3 layers at 0.2.
Extra per tile
0 g, +6 min (H2C); +1.6 g, +8 min (H2D hybrid).
Looks●●●●●
Printability●●●○○
Hides seams●●●●●

The strongest optical pattern of the set and the best camouflage after terrazzo: the eye is busy resolving cubes and never finds a seam. It also makes the bores look like holes drilled through a solid block. The price is a third tone; on the H2D that tone comes from relief and light rather than a third filament, which works in a lit room and flattens in a dark one.

Chosen over a hexagon lattice because the hex is already the Hex rack's identity and the Cells page covers lattice ridges. Triangles give a flat, equal-area two-colour field that the seams cross along a triangle edge (column seams) or across a row of apexes (row seams); at 1.0 mm the gap is lost in the colour edges. Where the collar cuts the field it leaves a ring of half-triangles, which is the one place this pattern looks accidental; the 2 mm plain band is there to tidy that.

07 · truchet arcs

TruchetSixty-four seeded quarter-circle cells per tile, continuous across every seam

Family
Smith-Truchet tiling: each square cell carries two quarter-arcs in one of two orientations.
Made
Flat inlay, face-down; also good as a 1 mm ridge relief, and as a through-cut lattice on the lit variant (the loops become light channels).
Registration
Pitch-registered: cell P/8 = 13.85 mm, arcs r 6.93; one deterministic seed. Clearance 1.5 mm then a 2 mm collar at r 52.
Colours
2.
Filament
PETG-CF black with PETG HF white (shown); walnut with marble.
Min feature
1.6 mm arcs; closest approach between arcs 2.2 mm.
Extra per tile
0 g, +4 min.
Looks●●●●○
Printability●●●●○
Hides seams●●●●●

Truchet is the geometric answer to "random but ordered": every cell is one of two states, yet the arcs chain into loops and paths that never break, including across seams, because the cell grid is the pitch grid and arcs leave each cell at its edge midpoints. Nine identical tiles in four rotations look like nine different ones. The bore simply interrupts loops, which Truchet patterns tolerate better than any other here.

The quatrefoil is the node figure with the strongest "designed" presence per gram, and it is the direct geometric cousin of the current four-petal bloom. At 1.5 mm outline that line is a quarter of the line width and reads as a drawn axis, but it is visible.

09 · sashiko

SashikoHishi diamond stitch grid, six lines per pitch, gaps at every crossing

Family
Japanese sashiko, hishi (diamond) grid in running stitch.
Made
Flat inlay, face-down (stitches are first-layer islands, anchored by the body printed over them).
Registration
Pitch-registered: lines at 45 degrees with intercepts every P/6 = 18.47 mm, stitch period 4.354 mm (three per lattice span), dash offset so every crossing falls in a gap. Clearance 1.5 mm then a 2 mm collar at r 52.
Colours
2.
Filament
PETG-CF black (indigo stand-in) with PETG HF white stitches; walnut with marble.
Min feature
Stitches 2.9 x 1.2 mm, about 600 small islands per tile: widen to 1.4 if any fail to stick on the first layer; textured PEI helps.
Extra per tile
0 g, +6 min (many short first-layer moves).
Looks●●●●○
Printability●●●○○
Hides seams●●●○○

Sashiko reads as cloth from a distance and as geometry up close. The lattice is period-locked and the dash phase is computed from the lattice vertices, so a stitch never straddles a seam at a different length on the two sides. It is the most fragile first layer on this page: hundreds of 1.2 mm islands, each printed before the body covers it. The textured plate and a slow first layer make it routine; a smooth plate would not.

12 · line interference

MoireRings about the bore crossing rings about the nodes

Family
Op-art / line interference (two concentric families).
Made
Flat inlay, face-down (the merging of crossing lines is a union of first-layer paths, which the slicer handles). Not a relief: raised 1 mm lines crossing at 5 degrees would blob.
Registration
Node rings r 3 to 48 and bore rings r 55 to 80, both 1.0 mm on a 3.0 mm pitch. Node rings are shared by four tiles and continue exactly; bore rings mirror at the seams. Clearance band r 53.5, collar 2 mm at r 52.
Colours
2.
Filament
Walnut with marble (the fleck in both softens the bands); PETG-CF black with white for the full op-art effect.
Min feature
1.0 mm lines, 2.0 mm gaps; where lines cross at under 10 degrees they fuse into 2 to 4 mm patches, intended.
Extra per tile
0 g, +6 min.
Looks●●●●○
Printability●●●●○
Hides seams●●●○○

Both families are circles about points the rack already has, so the interference figure is the rack's own geometry made visible: hyperbolic bands between each bottle and each node. The node rings hide the seams because four tiles share them. The bore rings do not: at mid-side they meet their neighbour's rings in mirror image, which reads as a crease. It is the most unusual option here and the one that most needs a printed sample before judging.

14 · Recommendation

Top three

1. Girih

The geometry of the grid itself: stars on the nodes, bottles in the crosses, and the seams turned into straps. Two colours, 1.5 mm lines, nothing under the minimum, zero extra material. It is the one pattern here that looks more considered the closer you get.

2. Truchet

Best seam-hiding of the geometric set, two colours, and nine identical tiles that read as nine different ones through rotation alone. Also the only one that converts directly to a through-cut lattice for the lit variant. Ordered randomness that a geometry-minded eye will enjoy decoding.

3. Blocks

The showpiece if the H2C is available: three tones, the strongest optical depth, and the bores become drilled holes in a solid. On the H2D the third tone comes from a 0.6 mm relief, which works in daylight and flattens at night, so print one tile both ways before deciding.

Close behind: Stepped fan for a dark, deco-leaning room (ties on score with Girih), Fret if the preference is classical and two-colour, and Chevron as the quiet, wood-floor option that prints in a single colour.

Before modelling: all twelve are flat-inlay candidates, so one face-down test tile in the chosen filament pair settles the surface question for the whole page. Print it with the Girih star on one corner and a Truchet cell row on the other and look at both under the room light.
