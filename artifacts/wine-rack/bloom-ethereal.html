Bloom wine rack · companion note 4 · ethereal front-plate variants

Made of lightnine soft fronts for the same tiles, in one or two filaments

Ethereal here is made from form and light, not from colour: translucent filament over the COB strips the rack already has, thickness that lets backlight draw a picture, hairline grooves and ridges, glow and sheen. Lit versions are drawn on a night ground with the same tile beside them, lights off. Three of them are honestly nothing without power, and the page says which.

Constraints. These tiles are 3D printed, so there are no colour fades: no gradient spools, no halftone or dithered tone, no blended filaments. A printer lays down a colour or it does not. Where a variant wants a softer edge it uses discrete stepped bands, or it lets the shape do it (thickness, grooves, ridges) and light the rest. Every tile is one or two colours by default and never more than three, because each colour beyond the two nozzles of an H2D means an AMS swap with a purge on every layer that carries it, which can double a tile's print time and waste more filament than the tile weighs; two colours print one per nozzle with near-zero purge. Every variant states its colours per tile and per rack.

This revision of the draft dropped Breath (a halftone fade from the bore) and Aurora (a gradient-spool body), reworked Mist into stepped bands, Nimbus into solid outlined clouds and Pearl into a single silk, removed the fading ring opacity from Ripple, and added Lantern.

0 · How light gets into a tile

Three light paths, and what each can draw

Edge light (what the design has). The tile's back carries an 11 x 3.2 mm rebate along its top and bottom edges for a 10 mm 24 V COB strip that runs along the two internal row seams. Light enters the tile through those edges and travels inside it as in a light guide. A translucent tile glows softly all over, brightest in a 20 mm band along the lit edges, and anything that interrupts the guide leaks light toward the viewer: the 33.7 degree flare (a bright ring around every bottle, for free) and any groove or dot cut into the back. Edge light draws engraved things, and it draws them in one filament.

Back light (needs one change). A lithophane, where face thickness between 0.8 and 3 mm sets the brightness, only works with light behind it. Behind each lobe sits the solid PETG-CF front ring, so a true lithophane needs a pocket in the ring under each lobe with a short COB segment or LED filament in it. One variant asks for that; the rest use the existing strips or none.

No light. Daylight ethereal comes from the surface and the material: fine relief and hairlines that catch a window, a frosted face from the textured plate or 0.08 mm layers, the mirror sheen of a single silk filament, glass-fleck sparkle, and phosphorescent PLA that holds a faint glow for half an hour after the room goes dark. Fuzzy skin works on walls only in Bambu Studio, so it frosts the bore and flare (a wall) but not the flat face.

Print rules as before: 0.8 mm minimum feature, shapes clear of r 53 from the bore centre or resolved by a collar ring, face-down for first-layer inlay (crisp two-colour edges, no layer lines), face-up at 0.08 to 0.12 mm where relief or silk sheen matters.

Filaments used, and which are off-brief

  • Bambu PLA Translucent (frosted, the diffuser) and PETG Translucent (clear): in the brief for the lit variant.
  • PLA Marble, Panchroma / Bambu PLA Matte, PETG HF white: in the brief.
  • PLA Silk+ pearl (one colour): off-brief because silk is brittle with weak layer bonding. Fine here because a tile carries no load: it is pinned to the front ring and nothing bears on it.
  • PLA Glow: off-brief (abrasive, stiff). Fine on the stock hardened nozzle; keep it out of an AMS Lite. Charges under room light, glows green-white for 30 to 60 min.
  • PLA Galaxy: off-brief only in that the brief did not list it; glass flecks, mildly abrasive, prints like PLA Basic. Loadless tile, so fine.

01 · lithophane, back-lit · 1 colour

HaloFace thickness thins toward the bottle so backlight draws a ring of light around each onenothing without powerneeds ring pockets

Make
Lithophane relief carved into the back of a face-down tile: face 0.8 mm thick from r 52 to 56, then thickening on a smooth curve to 3.0 mm by r 72 and the tile edges. Lobes solid (100 % infill) so no infill shadow shows. Face flat, textured plate.
Colours
1 per tile, 1 per rack: Bambu PLA Translucent (frosted). PETG Translucent gives a sharper, less milky halo.
LED
Not the existing strips. A 6 mm deep pocket in the front ring under each corner lobe holding a 25 mm segment of 5 mm COB (24 V warm white) or 3 mm LED filament, wired through the post gap. Diffuser gap: at least 5 mm of air between LED and tile back, or the segment shows as a hot bar. Edge light alone makes this a plain Frost tile.
Min feature
0.8 mm face. Layers 0.1 mm on the lobes (variable layer height), 0.2 elsewhere.
Extra / tile
-2.4 g (carved back), +18 min (0.1 mm band).
Bore / seam
The thinnest, brightest band hugs the flare, so the bore gets the light. Seams run through the thick 3 mm corners, which stay dark lit and hide them.

The purest version of the brief: nine bottles in nine haloes and nothing else. It is also the one variant that changes the skeleton, so it is costed honestly as a ring modification, not a tile swap. With the lights off it is blank.

02 · edge-lit engraving · 1 colour

DandelionSeed heads engraved on the back of each lobe, lit by the strips the rack already hasnothing without power

Make
Face-down tile; 0.8 mm wide, 0.6 mm deep grooves engraved on the back: a 3 mm boss on the node, 14 radial stalks from r 6 to r 24 per lobe, each ending in a 1.2 mm seed dot. Grooves scatter the edge light forward.
Colours
1 per tile, 1 per rack: PLA Translucent (soft) or PETG Translucent (crisp bright lines).
LED
Existing 10 mm COB strips in the row-seam rebates, as designed; no diffuser gap, the tile is the diffuser. The rack's top and bottom edges have no strip; a third strip in the top rebate of the top row evens it out.
Min feature
0.8 mm grooves; 0.2 mm layers (grooves are on the top surface while printing, invisible from the front).
Extra / tile
0 g, +4 min.
Bore / seam
Stalks stop at r 24 from the node, well clear of the flare; the flare glows as a ring of its own. Seams pass through the seed heads where the eye expects a stalk, and the lit seams are where the heads are brightest.

Edge-lit acrylic signs work exactly this way, and a printed translucent tile is a thick, slightly milky acrylic. Because the seed heads sit on the nodes and the inner nodes sit on the lit seams, the brightest part of the front is the pattern itself. By day the ghost lines are faint; call it nothing without power.

FrostA plain frosted tile with breath rings engraved around each borenothing without power

Make
Face-down tile on the textured plate (the texture is the frost); rings 0.8 mm wide, 0.5 mm deep engraved on the back at r 53, 56, 59.5, 63.5. Fuzzy skin on the bore and flare walls (0.2 mm thickness) scatters more light into the bottle ring.
Colours
1 per tile, 1 per rack: PLA Translucent. PETG Translucent reads as glass and shows the skeleton behind it.
LED
Existing strips. No diffuser gap.
Min feature
0.8 mm grooves; 0.2 mm layers.
Extra / tile
0 g, +2 min.
Bore / seam
The bore is the feature: flare ring plus four rings. Seams are not hidden; they read as lit hairlines.

The baseline for every lit variant and the cheapest: two minutes a tile over the plain design. In the dark it is nine soft windows with a ring of light around each bottle. In daylight it is a white frosted tile with nothing on it.

04 · opaque tracery on a translucent tile · 2 colours

LanternA paper-cut tracery inlaid in opaque grey: a quiet pattern by day, a silhouette against the glow by night

Make
Face-down two-colour inlay, 1.0 mm deep (5 layers) so the grey is fully opaque. Lines 1.0 mm: rings at r 53 and r 64 with 36 spokes between, and on each node rings of r 5 and r 12 with spokes, one quarter per tile.
Colours
2 per tile, 2 per rack: body PLA Translucent (nozzle 1), tracery Panchroma Matte grey or PLA Matte charcoal (nozzle 2). One per nozzle on the H2D, near-zero purge.
LED
Existing strips in the row-seam rebates; no diffuser gap.
Min feature
1.0 mm lines, 0.2 mm layers.
Extra / tile
0 g, +6 min (5 inlay layers with nozzle changes).
Bore / seam
The r 53 ring is the collar, so the flare ends on a line. Seams cut each node rosette into its four quarters, mirror-matched, so the cut sits on a spoke.

Paper lanterns and pierced-tin shades: the pattern is in the dark parts, and light does the rest. It is the one lit variant that is still a finished object with the power off, and it stays at two colours because the second colour only has to block light, not make tone.

05 · stepped bands, daylight · 2 colours

MistThree stepped white bands along every tile edge, so the seams sit inside the haze

Make
Face-down two-colour inlay. From the tile edge: 4.0 mm solid white, 2.0 mm gap, 1.6 mm line, 2.4 mm gap, 0.8 mm line; corner radii 4 / 9 / 13 mm. 1.5 mm collar ring at r 52.
Colours
2 per tile, 2 per rack: body Panchroma Matte pale blue-grey (or Bambu PLA Matte Sky Blue), bands PETG HF white or PLA Marble.
LED
None.
Min feature
0.8 mm hairline; 0.2 mm layers.
Extra / tile
0 g, +4 min.
Bore / seam
At the seam two 4 mm white bands meet, so the 0.3 mm gap is a hairline inside an 8.3 mm white bar. The bore sits in clear blue with a white collar; at mid-side the band and the collar leave under 0.8 mm of blue, which is merged into one white band in CAD.

The draft's Mist dissolved the seams with a dot screen whose dots grew toward the edge, which is a dithered fade. This version keeps the idea and does it in three hard steps, like a stepped mat around a print. It is still the best seam-hiding of the four studies by construction, and nothing on it is finer than 0.8 mm.

Colours
2 per tile, 2 per rack: body PLA Matte pale blue, clouds PETG HF white.
LED
None.
Min feature
0.8 mm outline and gap; 0.2 mm layers.
Extra / tile
0 g, +5 min.
Bore / seam
Clouds pass behind the bore, cleared to r 54 and closed with a 1.5 mm white collar, so the bottle sits in front of the sky. Seams cross clouds at random points and vanish in the white.

The draft dithered a cloud field into dots of varying size, which is a halftone fade. Drawn as flat clouds with a traced edge it becomes a picture rather than a tone, it prints as two clean regions per layer, and it is calmer at a metre.

07 · glow-in-the-dark points · 2 colours

ConstellationSeeded stars in phosphorescent PLA on a sparkle body: sparkle by day, a faint sky by night, no wiring

Make
Face-down two-colour inlay. 46 seeded points per tile (1.0 to 1.8 mm) on a seed wrapped to the pitch so density does not change at the seams; three short 0.8 mm connecting lines; a 2 mm collar.
Colours
2 per tile, 2 per rack: body Bambu PLA Galaxy dark blue, stars and collar Bambu PLA Glow. Both off-brief, both fine for a loadless tile on the hardened nozzle.
LED
None. The glow charges from room light; a UV strip in the existing rebates would recharge it on demand.
Min feature
1.0 mm dots, 0.8 mm lines; 0.2 mm layers. Glow PLA prefers a slow first layer.
Extra / tile
0 g, +4 min.
Bore / seam
Stars kept outside r 54 with the collar as the bore's edge. Random points hide seams completely.

Two faces and no power: a dark sparkling field by day and a fading star map at night, with every bottle ringed in the same glow. The glow is soft and short-lived; that is phosphorescence, and it is part of the charm.

08 · relief hairlines, daylight · 2 colours (or 1)

RippleRings spreading from every bottle at widening pitch, meeting their neighbours' rings at the seams

Make
Relief up, face-up: 1.0 mm rings 0.5 mm high (4 layers at 0.12) on an ironed matte face, r 53 to the corners. Single-colour version: rings in the body filament, shadow does the drawing.
Colours
2 per tile, 2 per rack: body Panchroma Matte pale grey, rings PLA Silk+ pearl or PLA Marble. Or 1 (all grey, or all pearl).
LED
None. Option: the same rings engraved on the back of a PLA Translucent tile, edge-lit from the existing strips (then 1 colour, and nothing without power).
Min feature
1.0 mm rings; 0.12 mm layers on the relief band.
Extra / tile
+0.9 g, +8 min.
Bore / seam
The first ring at r 53 is the collar. Seams cross rings where they fall; only at the mid-sides do they become part of the picture, where two ring systems meet.

Fine relief is what catches a window: at 0.5 mm the rings throw hairline shadows that move as you walk past. The draft drew the outer rings fading out; a printed ring does not fade, so every ring is now drawn the same and only the widening pitch makes them thin out.

PearlRadial hairline flutes in one pearl silk, so each ridge catches the light at its own angle

Make
Relief up, face-up at 0.08 mm: 1.0 mm ridges 0.4 mm high every 4 degrees from r 53.5 to the tile edge, body and ridges one filament, single nozzle, no colour change.
Colours
1 per tile, 1 per rack: Bambu PLA Silk+ pearl white. Optional 2: body in a matching PLA Matte so only the ridges shine. Off-brief silk is fine: no load.
LED
None.
Min feature
1.0 mm ridges, 2.7 mm apart at the collar; 0.08 mm layers on the relief band.
Extra / tile
+1.6 g, +12 min.
Bore / seam
Ridges radiate from the bore and start at a 1.5 mm collar. Seams cut ridges at an angle; four fans meet at each node like a starburst.

The draft used a dual-colour silk whose hue shifts ridge to ridge, which puts two or more colours in every strand and reads as a colour blend. A single pearl silk keeps the effect that matters, a field of tiny mirrors turned at ninety angles, and drops to one filament. Silk needs the slow 0.08 mm band and strings; it is the most demanding print here.

All nine tiles of a rack are the same file, so colours per rack equal colours per tile; no variant needs a third colour. Bracketed values are the alternative make. Grams are carved or relief volume at 1.24 g/cm3; minutes are extra layers, finer layers and nozzle changes on an H2D at 0.4 mm against the 38 g, 48 min base tile. Lit / Unlit are looks ratings in each state; - means the variant has no light source (a daylight tile that simply goes dark at night). Constellation's lit score is its own glow. Nothing has been printed; ratings are a designer's estimate. Rendered glow is a simulation: real edge-lit brightness falls off faster than drawn, about half by 30 mm from the strip in frosted PLA.

Lit versus unlit, honestly

  • Nothing without power: Halo (a blank frosted tile by day), Frost (blank, rings barely visible) and Dandelion (faint ghost lines only). Choose these only if the rack is lit whenever it is looked at.
  • Ethereal with the lights off: Mist, Nimbus, Ripple and Pearl (daylight tiles that go dark at night like any shelf), Constellation (sparkle by day, its own glow by night) and Lantern (grey tracery on frost).
  • Both states well: Lantern and Constellation. Ripple engraved on a translucent tile is a third, but then it is single-colour and blank by day.
  • LED layout: Dandelion, Frost and Lantern use the two 10 mm COB strips in the existing row-seam rebates with no diffuser gap; the tile is the diffuser. The rack's top and bottom edges have no strip; a third strip in the top rebate of the top row (and the bottom of the bottom row) evens the glow for one more 335 mm length. Halo needs ring pockets with short COB segments and at least 5 mm of air behind the tile. The rest need no wiring.
11 · Recommendation

Top three

1. Lantern

The only lit variant that is still finished with the power off: grey tracery on frost by day, a dark lace against the glow by night. Two colours, one per nozzle, existing strips, no diffuser gap, zero extra grams, 1.0 mm lines.

2. Dandelion

The lit showpiece in a single filament: the strips are already there, the seed heads sit on the lit seams, and it costs four minutes a tile. Pick it if the rack will be lit in the evenings; by day it is a near-blank frosted tile.

3. Mist

The daylight answer: the best seam-hiding of all four studies, two on-brief filaments, three hard steps instead of a fade, nothing under 0.8 mm, and the easiest print on the page after Frost.

Also: Constellation for two faces and no wiring (two off-brief but harmless spools); Halo if the front ring will be pocketed, because lit it is the most beautiful thing on the page; Pearl for a window-lit room, where one silk spool earns its trouble.

Test prints before modelling (one plate, about an hour):

  1. Lithophane thickness calibration strip, PLA Translucent, 0.6 to 3.2 mm in 0.2 mm steps at 0.1 mm layers, 100 % infill, held over a COB offcut at 3, 5 and 8 mm: sets the Halo curve and the diffuser gap.
  2. Translucency sample, 40 x 40 x 8 mm in PLA Translucent and PETG Translucent, edge-lit from one side, with 0.8 mm back grooves at 5, 15, 30 and 45 mm: real brightness fall-off, and frost versus glass for Dandelion and Frost.
  3. Lantern opacity card, face-down, grey inlay 0.4, 0.6, 1.0 and 1.4 mm deep on PLA Translucent, over the same COB: the shallowest inlay that reads as a solid silhouette.
  4. Hairline card, face-down, 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.0 mm lines and dots in matte blue + white and Galaxy + Glow: smallest feature that sticks, and how long the Glow lasts.
  5. Silk swatch face-up at 0.08 mm with ten ridges: confirms Pearl's sheen and stringing.
