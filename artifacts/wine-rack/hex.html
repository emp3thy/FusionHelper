01Concept

The modern brief asks for a confident single idea. Hex Eight has one: a pointy-top hexagonal ring, 110 mm across the flats, 30 mm deep, printed lying on its show face. Repeat it sixteen times, lock the copies together with identical bow-tie keys, stand the lot on a plinth rail, and the rack exists.

It belongs to the WineHive and Torsten Johansson lineage, a repeated honeycomb cell rather than a cabinet with holes, and to the Koziol module in scale: the whole rack is a 330 x 323 x 180 mm block, almost exactly Koziol's 36 x 35 cm footprint. What makes it Bauhaus rather than decorative is that nothing is added: the chamfer that gives the show face a crisp edge is the same chamfer that becomes a 2 mm V reveal at every seam, the joint is invisible, and the base rail is the only line that is not a hexagon.

Why hex and not a 3 x 3 square grid

A square grid at the same 110 pitch holds nine bottles; the 3-2-3 honeycomb holds eight. That is the whole cost, one bottle (11 %). What it buys:

  • Support geometry. A pointy-top cell is a 120-degree V. Every diameter from 74 to 96 mm seats on two lines of contact and centres itself; nothing rolls and no separate saddle, peg or cradle is needed. A square cell needs a flat floor (bottles roll) or a cradle insert.
  • Load path. The middle row sits in the Vs of the bottom row: its weight goes into the diagonal flats as bearing, through the hoop of a flat-printed ring (load in XY, the proven honeycomb architecture). Keys only stop the lattice spreading; no printed member is in bending across layers.
  • Looks. One cell, one key, one rail. The 14 mm web between bottles is two 7 mm walls meeting under a V reveal, so the front face reads as a drawn honeycomb, not a block with holes; the open corners of the 3-2-3 are the honeycomb's honest edge against the cube.

05Joints and tolerances

Double-dovetail key

  • Bow-tie section: neck 10 at the interface, base 12.9, 4.0 mm into each wall, flank 20 degrees from the depth axis. Derived: dt_base = dt_neck + 2 dt_d tan(dt_ang).
  • Groove = key offset outward by dt_gap 0.25 mm per side (brief: sliding dovetail 0.20-0.30): opening 10.53, base 13.63, depth 4.25. Leaves 2.75 mm of wall behind the groove.
  • Engagement 27 mm per ring (30 ring minus 3 floor). Keys are 174 long (rack_d - 2 floor_t) and span the 120 mm between plates as the rack's only depth members: 19 of them, in a space frame.
  • Keyed, no backwards: the key is symmetric end to end and side to side, the ring is symmetric when flipped, so there is no wrong orientation. Grooves are blind at the show face, so a key can only enter from inside the rack and cannot leave once both plates are on.
  • Rings are the same part in both plates: back plate rings face the front, front plate rings face the back (flip the same print).

Tolerance coupon

The script builds hex_coupon_block (30 x 20 x 30 block with one blind groove on its long face) and hex_coupon_key (40 mm key). Print both first, on the ring profile.

07Assembly and retention

  1. Print and test the coupon (section 05). Then plates A-G. Deburr nothing; wipe the groove mouths.
  2. Lay rail 1 show face down. Stand 6 keys in its grooves (each key half in the rail, half proud).
  3. Drop the three bottom rings onto the rail keys, flares down. Stand 2 keys between neighbouring rings (vertical flats), 4 keys in the upper-inner flats.
  4. Drop the two middle rings on; add 1 key between them and 4 keys in their upper flats. Drop the three top rings on. Back plate done: 8 rings, 19 keys standing 144 mm proud, all 13 ring-ring and 6 ring-rail joints made.
  5. Place rail 2 and the eight front rings show face up over the key ends, one at a time, bottom row first. The tapered opening (0.25 mm clearance plus the flanks) self-centres; press down by hand until each seats on its 3 mm floor.
  6. Stand the block up on its rails. Stick four rubber door bumpers under the rails if the cube floor is slick.
  7. Slide the rack to the back of the cube. 332 envelope in a 335 opening: 1.5 mm each side, 11.5 mm above. It is retained by its own 2 kg and footprint (180 deep, 330 wide), the cube walls stop any tipping, and 12 kg of bottles pin it. Bottles go in base first; the flare takes a 101 mm entry down to the 96 mm slot.

09Risks and open questions

Cube depth is unknown. The 180 mm rack assumes a ~390 mm KALLAX-class cube so a 320 mm bottle ends near the front. In a shallower cube the necks protrude; set rack_d to (cube depth - 150) down to 150 mm minimum. Below 150 the front ring moves onto Burgundy shoulders and bottles tilt neck-down.
Fit coupon first. Nineteen keys at 0.25 mm per side: a 0.1 mm printer offset turns "slides home" into "mallet". Print the coupon on the ring profile before any ring, and retune dt_gap. The front plate must engage up to six keys at once; the groove opening and 20-degree flanks give 0.25 mm of lead-in, which is not much.
Creep. Bottle load is bearing on 7 mm walls over 30 mm (well under 0.1 MPa); the only slender members are the keys, which are unloaded in service. The risk is the rails: 12 kg on two 6 mm plinths at the ring vertices. PETG-CF at 5 walls is fine; in PETG HF raise base_t to 8.
Chamfer features on hex edges. Fusion chamfers on straight-edged loops are routine, but the 1 mm reveal meets 120-degree corners at the zig-zag rail; if the rail chamfer errors in the timeline, drop it to 0.6 mm (ch_reveal only on the rail is a 1-line change) or let elephant-foot compensation do the job.
Pulled-forward bottles. A bottle pulled 40 mm out of the cell moves its CoG past the front ring and tips. Push bottles home; the back wall of the cube is the stop.
Rails print on the diagonal. 330 mm at 37.8 wide is inside the 121 mm diagonal rule, but Bambu Studio's auto-arrange will not find it: rotate 45 degrees by hand and check the plate view, brim on, bed edge temperature is the least uniform.
